# Governance assessment

> Define who may decide, operate, restrict and review an AI system

**Goal:** Convert organizational AI policy into decision rights, required evidence and operational controls for a specific system.

## Procedure

1. Identify applicable internal policies, data-use rules, sector obligations and governance frameworks.
2. Determine the use-case risk tier using your organization's method. Preserve the rationale and any uncertainty.
3. Assign the people or committees authorized to accept residual risk and approve production scope.
4. Identify mandatory control gates: privacy, security, fairness, human oversight, performance, supplier assurance, etc.
5. Define review independence requirements. Where risk is high, the implementer and acceptance authority should not be the same unchallenged person.
6. Set evidence expectations for every gate, including what counts as sufficient and current evidence.
7. Establish escalation for missing evidence, blocked controls, incidents and time-bound policy exceptions.
8. Confirm how an approved restriction will be enforced and tested in the operational system.

## Decision-rights matrix (example)

| Action | Accountable person/function | Review participants |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Register intended use | Business owner | Technical owner |
| Set critical control criteria | Governance lead | Risk, legal, security, engineering |
| Verify evaluation evidence | TEVV/evidence reviewer | Independent assurance reviewer |
| Accept material residual risk | Delegated acceptance authority | Control owners and risk reviewers |
| Suspend unsafe use | Designated incident/operational authority | System owner, governance |

## Common mistakes

Do not equate system-admin rights with organizational approval authority. Do not assume a named reviewer has reviewed a record just because they can view it. Ensure policy exceptions have owners, expiry conditions and a review date.

**Output:** A governance charter for the system and a list of required assurance controls.
