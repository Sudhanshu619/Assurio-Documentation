# Risk scoring

> An illustrative way to make risk discussion consistent

Assurio describes risk and impact assessment but does **not** publicly specify a universal 1–5 scoring formula. The matrix below is an **example organizational rubric**. Replace it with your adopted risk policy and applicable legal standards.

## Example ordinal dimensions

| Score | Severity (example) | Likelihood (example) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Negligible, readily reversible | Rare under expected conditions |
| 2 | Minor, localized | Unlikely |
| 3 | Meaningful operational or individual impact | Possible |
| 4 | Major, significant rights/financial/safety exposure | Likely |
| 5 | Critical or potentially irreversible harm | Frequent / highly probable |

A simple *severity × likelihood* score can help triage, but it is not a validated physical measurement. A low-probability catastrophic event may require a critical gate regardless of its product score.

## Example policy bands (illustrative)

| Product | Illustrative label | Suggested response |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1–4 | Low | Standard review and monitoring |
| 5–9 | Moderate | Named control owners and scheduled review |
| 10–16 | High | Independent review and restrictions |
| 17–25 | Critical | Escalation to delegated high-risk authority; no unreviewed use |

## Recording residual risk

Always state controls, evidence of effectiveness, unresolved uncertainty and the person authorized to accept the remainder. Do not publish these thresholds as Assurio defaults or as a guarantee of compliance.
